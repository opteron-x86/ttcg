Reading the evidence

What this audit measures

The charts describe the current catalog. Changing the scope changes the distribution charts and explorer. Pack coverage always examines the complete eligible pack pool; filtering the catalog does not change what a pack contains.

Acquisition is separate from the released flag

Routes include starter grants, authored NPC collections, standard packs, tournament packs, individual-card merchants and tournament trophy candidacy. Merchants generate ordinary tier I–VII cards; player-sold uniques are resale only. Trophy candidates have the released flag turned off in the catalog but can already be awarded by tournaments. A candidate is not guaranteed to appear: reputation, the event class, world access, deaths and existing ownership can restrict it. NPC collections describe starting ownership, before trades or replenishment.

Acquisition prose such as “caravan traders” is displayed as an unimplemented note when it has no corresponding route. Development grants and arbitrary console additions are excluded.

Stronger layouts and shared layouts

A stronger peer has the same tier, no lower printed side in any position, and at least one higher side. Cards are not rotated. Creature groups, affinities, rarity and access may make the apparently weaker layout useful; Reverse changes the preference and Same/Plus reward particular numbers. Identical printed layouts do not mean identical cards. These flags are prompts for review, not removal recommendations.

Edge comparisons are not match simulations

Each card is compared with every other identity in its tier, over the four opposing directions. Basic counts strictly larger sides, Reverse strictly smaller sides, and equal sides count as neither. All peers, including trophy candidates, are weighted equally. These figures ignore board position, hand construction, starting player, Affinity, Legion, Decimation and combos. They must not be read as game win rates.

Same/Plus candidates are shown through their printed layouts and the existing profile roles. Legion and Decimation activate when the third card of a creature group is placed, then modify that group by one. Catalog size measures options, not trigger probability in a played hand. Full-match simulations, including the six planned rules, belong to the later balance phase.

Pack coverage

Standard packs contain four Commons and a premium slot. An upgrade can move one slot into tiers IV–VI. Tournament packs roll five tier III–VII identities: 30% Common, 45% Rare and 25% Epic, with a 5% foil chance per card. The standard premium slot is 96.5% Rare and 3.5% Epic. Legendary uniques come from opponents and trophies. Each pack excludes repeated identities. A bucket with fewer identities than the maximum possible draws can run out during generation. The listed average draws are nominal roll expectations before duplicate exclusions and failures, not measured output odds. Unfinished superior candidates are excluded from packs, opponents and trophies.

Review proposals

Tier describes a strength band. The roster workshop gives Rare and Epic cards stronger placements, added strength or a standout edge within that band. Creature groups support coherent play; foil is cosmetic. Proposals do not modify the source catalog or recalculate these baseline charts. Exported reviews include the original values and catalog fingerprint. Imports merge proposals, replacing matching card IDs, and reject a different source snapshot so old decisions cannot silently overwrite a changed catalog.

The template builder checks ranks against the existing Python validator, including peak-side restrictions, and shows projected roster counts. This audit continues to describe the current catalog. Its review form reports the weighted budget; the Python validator remains authoritative for all profile restrictions.
